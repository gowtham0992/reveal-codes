PLAINTIFF'S RESPONSE IN OPPOSITION TO DEFENDANT'S MOTION TO DISMISS

Plaintiff Dana Whitfield, representing herself, asks the Court to deny Defendant's motion to dismiss under Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(b)(6).

I. BACKGROUND

I rented an apartment from Defendant from 2022 until 2025. After I reported mold and a broken heater to the city housing inspector and asked for a reasonable accommodation for my disability, Defendant raised my rent and told me it would not renew my lease. My complaint alleges that Defendant did this to punish me for asking for repairs and for an accommodation, which the Fair Housing Act forbids. 42 U.S.C. § 3617.

II. LEGAL STANDARD

A complaint survives a motion to dismiss if it contains enough facts to state a claim that is plausible on its face. Ashcroft v. Iqbal, 556 U.S. 662, 678 (2009). Because I am not a lawyer, my complaint must be read liberally. Haines v. Kerner, 404 U.S. 519, 520 (1972) ("less stringent standards than formal pleadings drafted by lawyers").

III. ARGUMENT

A. My complaint states a retaliation claim.

The Fifth Circuit has held that a tenant states a claim for retaliation under the Fair Housing Act when the landlord refuses to renew a lease within six months after the tenant asks for an accommodation. Alvarez v. Cascade Residential Mgmt., LLC, 1047 F.3d 212, 219 (5th Cir. 2019). The court explained that timing alone is enough at the pleading stage. Id. at 221. Courts in this district have denied motions to dismiss on facts like mine. Brennan v. Harbor Point Apartments, 1012 F. Supp. 2d 88, 95 (N.D. Tex. 2015).

B. I did not have to make my request in writing.

Defendant says I never gave written notice of my disability. But a landlord's duty to accommodate begins when the landlord knows of the disability, whether or not the request is in writing. Okafor v. Lindqvist Props., 912 F. 4th 33, 41 (5th Cir. 2023).

C. I should be allowed to gather evidence.

The Supreme Court has said that "a pro se plaintiff who alleges retaliation is entitled to discovery before a motion to dismiss may be granted." Iqbal, 556 U.S. at 679. If the Court finds any part of my complaint insufficient, I ask for leave to amend under Fed. R. Civ. P. 15(a)(2).

IV. CONCLUSION

For these reasons, I ask the Court to deny Defendant's motion to dismiss.
